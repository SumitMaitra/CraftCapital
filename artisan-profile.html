The Artisan's Story

Ranjit Kumar Das is a third-generation artisan from the historic craft village of Panchmura. He learned the intricate art of molding the iconic Bankura horse from his father when he was just ten years old. For decades, his family supplied these terracotta figures to local villages for ritualistic offerings during local festivals.

As the demand for authentic Indian handicrafts grew in urban centers, Ranjit became one of the pioneers in his cluster to adapt traditional designs for modern spaces. Recognizing the power of collective bargaining and shared resources, he played a crucial role in strengthening the local Self-Help Group (SHG) network, which now ensures fair wages and access to better kilns for over 50 families.

Today, Ranjit oversees a specialized workshop capable of handling significant institutional orders. His pieces are characterized by their robust firing, achieving a deep, rich terracotta red that is highly sought after by corporate gifting companies and boutique hotels alike. He is currently expanding his capacity to meet export interests from buyers in Japan and Europe.

"The clay tells us what it wants to become. We just guide it with our hands, honoring the techniques our ancestors taught us, while creating pieces that can travel the world."
