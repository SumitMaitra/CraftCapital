Ranjit Kumar Das: 30 Years of the Sacred Panchmura Horse

"Every horse is thrown in four hollow parts — neck, body, ears, and legs — then joined by hand before being sun-dried and fired in wood kilns. No two pieces are ever identical."

Ranjit's family has preserved the GI-tagged Panchmura craft technique for nearly a century. With CraftCapital, his SHG cooperative now ships directly to luxury hotels and corporate clients across India without intermediary markups.

Kamala Devi: Reviving 400-Year-Old Ghurni Clay Realism

"We harvest fine alluvial silt from the Jalangi river bed. It allows us to capture the delicate folds of a saree and realistic facial expressions on our miniature figurines."

Kamala leads a cooperative of 28 women sculptors in Krishnanagar. Her recent line of minimalist unglazed planters and festival sets combines modern architectural aesthetics with ancient river-clay durability.
