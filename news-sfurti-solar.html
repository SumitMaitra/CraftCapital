SFURTI Common Facility Centre in Bankura Goes Solar: 180 Potter Families Cut Monsoon Losses

✍️ By Subhashree Mukherjee • 📅 August 24, 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read
The newly upgraded solar-assisted drying facility at the Panchmura SFURTI Common Facility Centre, Bankura.

PANCHMURA, WEST BENGAL — For generations of terracotta potters in the Bankura district, the arrival of the monsoon season was accompanied by anxiety. Traditional unglazed terracotta pieces require 4 to 7 days of natural air drying before firing in wood kilns. High humidity frequently caused hairline fractures, ruining up to 40% of entire production batches.

This week, the Scheme of Fund for Regeneration of Traditional Industries (SFURTI) Common Facility Centre in Panchmura inaugurated a state-of-the-art 45kW solar-powered dehumidification and temperature-controlled drying tunnel.

"Earlier, when large institutional orders for Diwali came in August, we hesitated because rain could delay our kilns for weeks. Now, our drying cycles are reduced to just 18 hours with zero warping." — Ranjit Kumar Das, SHG Lead

The transition ensures that over 180 potter families registered under the cluster cooperative can guarantee year-round delivery schedules for hospitality chains and corporate buyers in Kolkata, Delhi, and Bengaluru.

The solar array also powers motorized clay pulverizing and pug mill machines, reducing manual labor for elderly artisans while tripling daily clay consistency benchmarks.
